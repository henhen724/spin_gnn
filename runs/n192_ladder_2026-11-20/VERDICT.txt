FINDING 116 -- WHAT ACTUALLY HAPPENED (from the NSPLIT probe, 53203962)

  At N=192 the model predicts the DEDUCED COLOUR with a teacher-forced
  cross-entropy of 1.3863 nats = ln(4) and an accuracy of 0.248 = 1/q,
  AT EVERY DENSITY AND ON BOTH SEEDS. Those two facts together pin it
  down: only the uniform distribution over q colours scores ln q. The
  model places equal probability on all four colours every time.

  The same quantity at N=96 is 0.0036 nats / 0.9991 accuracy.

  IT IS FLAT ACROSS DENSITY, which is the argument against "undertrained":
      rho      0.90      0.75      0.60      0.45      0.35
      n96_s0   0.000000  0.000062  0.000612  0.003318  0.007718

      n192_s1  1.386374  1.386336  1.386334  1.386312  1.386314
  At rho=0.90 nearly every neighbour is already a hint and the deduction
  is a one-hop lookup over five stated colours -- the easiest the task
  ever gets. An undertrained model is better on the easy end. This one
  is identical there.
