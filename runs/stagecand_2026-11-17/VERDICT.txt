WHAT THIS ANSWERS, AND WHAT IT LEAVES OPEN
  STATUS next-step 1 part (iii) said distinguishing deduction from
  'something else that agrees' needs a new instrument and not a refit.
  HALF OF THAT IS WRONG FOR THE THIRD TIME: the instrument was on disk.
  err_node plus a deterministic instance cache plus the j = 0 pre-state
  give the exact candidate set with no model, in six seconds.
  THE OTHER HALF IS RIGHT, AND NOW FOR A STATED REASON.  The instrument
  cannot separate the two accounts at this q and d, because the
  candidate count and the coloured-neighbour count are nearly
  COLLINEAR on these instances: an unforced cell with k = 4 has exactly
  two candidates 100.00% of the time and one with k = 3 does 95.96% of
  the time, so 'name the cell with the most assigned neighbours'
  reproduces 'name a near-forced cell' almost exactly.  Every positive
  signal here (C1, C4) survives the collinearity and dies under the
  control (C5).
  WHAT WOULD SEPARATE THEM IS A GENERATOR CHANGE, NOT A SCORER: an
  ensemble where k and c are decorrelated -- a larger q, or instances
  selected so that high-k cells often keep three or more candidates.
  THAT genuinely needs a job, and now the reason is structural and
  stated instead of 'nothing on disk distinguishes them'.
