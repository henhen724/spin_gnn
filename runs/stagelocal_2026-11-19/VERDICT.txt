WHAT THIS ANSWERS, AND WHAT IT LEAVES OPEN

  103 showed the colour step never writes an illegal colour.  This
  stage asks what computation that requires, and the answer is: the
  COMPLETE join, on essentially every emission.

  An emitter that joined against all but one of the named cell's
  assigned neighbours would be improper 12.5% of the time.  Over the
  24,008 wrong unforced emissions 103 measured on the 77 exact runs
  that is ~3011 expected violations.  Zero were observed, which bounds
  the join-completion rate at >= 0.9990 with 95% confidence.

  ACCOUNT (B1), GLOBAL INHIBITION, IS DEAD.  All four colours appear
  among the hints in 99.75% of instances, so 'this colour is visible in
  context' is true of every colour nearly always and cannot select the
  1-2 the model actually suppresses.  The suppressed set is cell-
  specific, and identifying it requires reading the named node's row of
  BEGIN_GRAPH and joining it against BEGIN_HINTS.

  ACCOUNT (B2), ADJACENCY-LOCAL INHIBITION, SURVIVES -- AND THAT WAS
  DISCLOSED.  Suppressing exactly the colours on this cell's neighbours
  IS computing the candidate set; the two are the same function and no
  output-only measurement can separate them.  What has changed is that
  the surviving account is now a specific, named, two-hop computation
  rather than 'some mechanism that agrees'.

  AND THE SLACK IS THE ARCHITECTURAL FINDING.  96.8% of named cells
  have at least one neighbour whose colour is duplicated on another
  neighbour, so a correct emitter does not need equal attention on all
  k.  For the smallest-analyzable-architecture brief that is the useful
  number: the join must be complete in EFFECT, not in EFFORT.
