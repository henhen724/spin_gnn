THE QUESTION HAS TWO ANSWERS AND THEY POINT OPPOSITE WAYS. Whether
the cell breaks the rule is an architecture property: all three seeds
clear the 0.01 bar, S1 HIT. HOW BADLY it breaks is a seed property
with an enormous effect: chi2 = 6641 on 2 d.o.f., p = 0, S2 MISS
by about as far as a homogeneity test can miss. The registered
hypothesis -- architecture, not seed -- is therefore right about the
failure and wrong about its size.

THE PUBLISHED d192 NUMBER IS THE BEST SEED OF THREE BY A FACTOR OF
7.0, and it was selected that way. S3 HIT: h_rule is 0.08106 for r68
against 0.5656 and 0.5641 for the two seeds that were never evaluated.
Seed 0 is in the 79 because it solved 1 instance of 400 at
rho = 0.50 while seeds 1 and 2 solved 0 of 400, and the gate is
solve_rate > 0. A gate on SOLVING turns out to be a gate on the RULE
arm, so the one admitted seed is the one that writes the forced
colour correctly, and 0.08106 is not a property of the cell. The
cell's typical seed sits at 0.565, and pooled over all three it is
0.3346 -- 4.13x the published figure, S5 MISS on the factor-of-2 half
of its statement while HITTING the half that matters for the bar.

THE TWO UNEVALUATED SEEDS AGREE WITH EACH OTHER ALMOST EXACTLY:
0.5656 [0.554, 0.578] and 0.5641 [0.552, 0.576], intervals OVERLAPPING.
So this is not a spread: it is two seeds in one mode and one seed in
another, which is the clump structure findings 130/131 described,
seen here at the level of a single cell. The minority clump is the
one that got published.

S4 MISSED, AND ITS DIRECTION IS THE SURPRISE. u = 1 - s_F(0) was
registered to rise with the worse seeds and it FELL: 0.4359 for r68
against 0.2882 for r80 and 0.2655 for r81. The two bad seeds name a
forced cell at the first emission MORE often than the good one, and
then write the wrong colour in it 57% of the time against 8%.
The two arms move in OPPOSITE directions across seeds of one cell, so
they are not two readings of one quality axis. Picking WHERE to write
and knowing WHAT to write are separable, and a seed can be better at
the first while being far worse at the second.

THE DENOMINATORS CARRY THE RESULT TOO. On identical instances r68
accumulated 12053 forced emissions against 6531 and 6745 -- 1.82x as many
-- because a rollout is censored at its first error and the bad seeds
die sooner. Survivorship therefore shrinks the very denominator that
a high h_rule is divided by, which means h_rule UNDERSTATES how
different these seeds are, not overstates it.

WHAT THIS CANNOT SAY. Three seeds of one cell at one width. It does
not show that seed dispersion is general -- and the contrast with
d320 says it is not: that cell's three seeds agreed INSIDE counting
noise (stage RULE8, SE_jack 1.196e-05 against SE_Poisson 1.747e-05)
while d192's three differ by 7.0x. If anything, seed dispersion is
itself width-dependent, and the small cell is the unstable one.
r80 and r81 are outside the 79 by construction and are never pooled
into any published fit; the correct use of them is the comparison
above, not a new population. No further training is needed to reach
this: the eval cost was one 3-minute GPU job on checkpoints that had
been sitting on scratch since 2026-11-10.
